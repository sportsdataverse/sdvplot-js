[deck.gl](https://deck.gl/) draws large data sets with WebGL: layers of points, arcs and polygons you can pan, zoom
and tilt, on a map or in your own coordinates. `@sportsdataverse/sdvplot/deckgl` builds the props for three of its
layers: team logos at points, arcs in team colours, and a sporty playing surface. It never imports deck.gl (install
`@deck.gl/core` and `@deck.gl/layers` 9.4 or later beside it, optional peers), so the props can be built anywhere, a
server included; pass them to the layer, as in `new IconLayer(logoIconLayer(rows, o))`. The props are plain objects:
the data rows, accessor functions that read them, and the layer's own settings. `surfaceLayer` draws through
`@sportsdataverse/sporty`, which this subpath imports when it loads. It is synchronous: load the league first
(`loadLeague`). The charts below are drawn by deck.gl in your browser: drag to pan and scroll to zoom.

The Python package builds the same props for pydeck (`sdvplot.pydeck`: `logo_icon_layer`, `pass_arc_layer` and
`surface_layer`). The two are held to one shared fixture of real rows and the props each must build from them.

## Shots on a rink

`surfaceLayer(sport, league, o)` is sporty's surface as `PolygonLayer` props: one data row per closed loop (a faceoff circle
is two rows, its half rings), in sporty's drawing order, filled in its colour (alpha included). It is in the surface's own coordinates, deck.gl's
`"cartesian"` coordinate system: feet for a rink and metres for a pitch, the origin at the centre. That is the frame
NHL api-web reports shots in, so they need no conversion. `o` goes to sporty's `surface()` (`updates`,
`colorUpdates`, `units`, ...). Text features (a football field's yard numbers) and hidden ones are left out. Draw it
in an `OrthographicView` with `flipY: false`, so y runs up as it does on the surface.

`logoIconLayer(rows, { x, y, team, league, size })` is `IconLayer` props with one logo per row at its `x` and `y`.
The logos are deck.gl's auto-packed icons: each data row's `icon` names the logo's URL, and deck.gl fetches each URL
once and packs it into its own atlas, 128 px tall at the logo's aspect, centred on the point. `size` is the logo's
height on screen in px (default 32). A team with no logo is left out with one warning, as the other adapters do. Over
a surface, give the icons `coordinateSystem: "cartesian"` too (this page's drawer does it for every layer).

## Passes as arcs, readable on the grass

`passArcLayer(rows, { x, y, xend, yend, team, league })` is `ArcLayer` props with one arc per row, from (`x`, `y`) to
(`xend`, `yend`). Its source end takes the team's primary colour and its target end the secondary (`which:
"secondary"` swaps them), so where the two colours differ the ends tell the direction; each team keeps one colour per
end. deck.gl's arcs stand
upright, which a top-down view sees as straight lines: `tilt: 90` lays them flat on the surface, and `height` (deck.gl's
`getHeight`, default 1) sets how far each curve swings; 0.3 keeps them inside the pitch.

Pass the colour the arcs are drawn on as `background` and every colour keeps at least 3:1 contrast against it, as in
dark mode. Each team's colour also aims to stay apart from the others' (a CIE76 ΔE of 20 or more), through sdvplot's
shared team palette (the Python package ports it, and the shared fixture holds both to every NBA, NFL, MLB and NHL
team); if no candidate is both readable and distinct, it keeps the readable colour and warns once. A team that does not
resolve takes `fallback` (default `#808080`) as given and stays out of the palette, so its contrast is not checked: pick
a fallback that reads on your background (the default grey has none on a `#808080` one). A row with a missing
coordinate is left out with one warning.

One caveat: a colour that does not read on the background gives way to the team's other colour. When only one of a
team's two colours reads, both ends take it, and the arcs no longer show their direction. France's colours are a navy
and a pale mint: on sporty's dark default grass only the mint reads, so every arc would be mint end to end. Here the
pitch is painted a mid-tone green (sporty's `colorUpdates`) on which both read, so each pass runs from navy at the
passer to mint at the receiver; the example checks that the two ends differ. Check `sourceColor` against
`targetColor` the same way when you pick a background.

## On a map

Without a surface, `x` and `y` are a longitude and a latitude: `logoIconLayer` and `passArcLayer` work on deck.gl's
default map view as they are, with their coordinate system left at deck.gl's default.

## Reading back what was drawn

There is no `drawnMarks` here: the props are the record. `props.data` holds each logo's `position`, `icon` (with its
`url`) and resolved `team` id, and each arc's `source`, `target` and `[r, g, b, a]` colours, with the input row's
`index`, so a test can check them without a WebGL context.
