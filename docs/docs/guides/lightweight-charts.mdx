[TradingView Lightweight Charts](https://www.tradingview.com/lightweight-charts/) draws time series on a canvas: price
lines with a crosshair, a price scale and a live last-value label, which suits a betting line moving or a win
probability swinging through a game. `@sportsdataverse/sdvplot/lightweight-charts` gives it team colours, prices read
as probabilities or odds, team logos at points, and the TradingView attribution its licence asks for. It never imports
lightweight-charts (install `lightweight-charts` 5.2.1 or later beside it, an optional peer), so everything can be
built anywhere, a server included; only `createChart(...)` needs a browser. It is synchronous: load the league first
(`loadLeague`). The chart below is drawn by Lightweight Charts in your browser.

## The TradingView attribution

Lightweight Charts is licensed under the Apache License 2.0, and its README (at 5.2.1) adds:

> This license requires specifying TradingView as the product creator.
> You shall add the "attribution notice" from the NOTICE file and a link to [https://www.tradingview.com/](https://www.tradingview.com/) to the page
> of your website or mobile application that is available to your users.

and that its `attributionLogo` chart option "will satisfy the link requirement". So a page that shows a chart needs
two things:

- **The link.** `withAttribution(chartOptions)` returns the options for `createChart` with `layout.attributionLogo` on:
  the library draws TradingView's logo on the chart, a link to tradingview.com. It is on by default. The options are
  copied, never changed. If your options turn the logo off (`layout: { attributionLogo: false }`), `withAttribution`
  turns it back on and warns once.
- **The notice.** `attributionNotice(container)` puts it on the page: a `<p>` appended to `container` (give it the
  element around the chart, `el.parentElement ?? el`), holding `TRADINGVIEW_NOTICE`, the attribution notice verbatim
  from the library's NOTICE file at 5.2.1, with its URL as a link:

  ```text
  TradingView Lightweight Charts™
  Copyright (с) 2025 TradingView, Inc. https://www.tradingview.com/
  ```

  It returns that `<p>`. Called again on the same container it returns the same notice and adds none, so a chart
  that is redrawn keeps one notice. On a server (no `document`) it does nothing.

## Team colours, and prices read as probabilities or odds

`teamSeriesOptions(team, { league, units })` is a LineSeries' options in the team's colour, for
`chart.addSeries(LineSeries, ...)`. `units` says what the prices are, and sets the series' `priceFormat`, which the
price scale, the crosshair label and the last-value label all read:

- `"probability"`: a fraction in [0, 1], read as a percentage ("58.5%", "100%");
- `"american"`: American odds, read with their sign ("+150", "-120"), rounded to whole points half away from zero
  (150.5 reads "+151", -150.5 reads "-151"); a price that rounds to zero reads "0", never "+0" or "-0".

Without `units`, Lightweight Charts formats the numbers itself. A team that does not resolve takes `fallback` (default
`#808080`) and warns.

`logoMarkers(rows, { x, y, team, league, height })` paints each row's logo centred on its `x` (a time, as in the series'
data) and `y` (a price on the series' scale), placed with the chart's own `timeToCoordinate` and `priceToCoordinate`,
`height` of the pane tall (a fraction in (0, 1], as in the Plotly, Vega-Lite and ECharts adapters; default 0.1).
Lightweight Charts' series markers draw only shapes and text, so the logos come from a series primitive: attach it with
`series.attachPrimitive(logos)`. With `units`, `logoMarkers` also checks the `y` values: a "probability" over 1 (58.46
rather than 0.5846) or American odds between -100 and +100 (1.91 is decimal odds) throw.

Building `logoMarkers` fetches nothing. The images start loading when it is attached, with
`crossOrigin = "anonymous"` so the canvas stays exportable, and the chart redraws as each one lands. A team with no logo
draws nothing and warns once, as the ECharts adapter does; an image that fails to load is never drawn (no broken image)
and warns once. A row at a time the series has no point for, or a price off the pane, is not drawn: keep the logos
inside the series' price range, or give the price scale room (`scaleMargins`).

Lightweight Charts' `time` must rise strictly. Plays that start on the same clock reading share a time, so the example
keeps the last play at each second.

## Dark mode, and several teams on one chart

Pass the chart's background as `background` and the colour keeps at least 3:1 contrast against it (WCAG's minimum for
graphics): a team colour under that gives way to the team's other colour, else is lightened (on a dark background) or
darkened until it reads. Pass every team on the chart as `teams`, in order, and each also stays apart from the earlier
ones (a CIE76 ΔE of 20 or more), so two teams never share a colour. Call `teamSeriesOptions` once per series with the
same `teams`. A colour that already reads and is distinct is kept; without `background` the colours are the teams' own.
A team that does not resolve never moves a real team's colour. Set the chart's own `layout.background` and
`layout.textColor` to match.

## Betting lines

`units: "american"` is there for a moneyline moving over the days before a game, with `logoMarkers` marking where it
opened or closed. The examples here hold no real line history yet, and they never invent one, so the gallery shows a win
probability only.

## Reading back what was drawn

`drawnMarks(logos)` returns `[team id, x, y, height, url]` for each logo the primitive last painted, `height` as a
fraction of the pane, the same shape as the other adapters' `drawnMarks`. A test can draw the chart (a browser, or jsdom
with a 2D context behind its canvases, as sdvplot's own tests do with the real library) and check which teams were
drawn where.
